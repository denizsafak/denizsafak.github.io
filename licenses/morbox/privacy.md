**Last Updated:** October 14, 2026

This Privacy Policy describes how Morbox ("we," "us," or "our"), published by Deniz Şafak, collects, uses, and shares information when you use our mobile application ("App").

> **In short**
> * Morbox is local-first: your files stay on your device.
> * Crash reports and usage statistics are optional, off by default, and you choose each one separately.
> * They go to Google Firebase and are linked only to an installation identifier.
> * You can change your choice at any time in Settings.
> * Contact: denizsafak98@gmail.com.

**Scope note:** This policy is written to cover the current release and planned future updates of Morbox. In the current release, crash reports (Firebase Crashlytics) and usage statistics (Google Analytics for Firebase) are optional and turned off by default. Nothing is collected until you choose to allow it, separately for each purpose, on the consent screen during onboarding or later in Settings. You can allow both, choose only one, or decline both, and you can change your choice at any time in Settings. If you skip onboarding, nothing is collected until you turn one of these settings on. Advertisements (Google AdMob) and paid subscriptions (Google Play Billing / Apple StoreKit) are **not present or active** in the current release; Sections 4 and 7 describe how those features will operate if and when they are introduced in future updates. Sections 5 (Analytics and Crash Diagnostics), 6 (ML Kit), and the Google Maps disclosures in Sections 8–9 describe functionality currently integrated in the App. The current release is distributed on Android; references to iOS describe functionality that applies if an iOS version is published.

## 1. Our Privacy Approach: Local-First
Our core design principle is local-first processing. Media conversion (video, audio, image, document, PDF), text recognition (OCR), background removal, and document scanning all run **on your device**. Your personal files are never uploaded to our servers for conversion — we operate no conversion servers and have no user accounts, so we never see your files.

Exceptions to purely local processing are limited to the following, each described below: (a) optional crash diagnostics and usage statistics (Section 5), which communicate with Google's Firebase infrastructure only if you allow them separately, on the onboarding consent screen or in Settings (and future advertising if introduced, Section 4); (b) one-time downloads of on-device ML models over the network (Section 6); (c) the in-app Privacy Policy / Terms screens, which fetch this policy text from our public documentation site so you always see the current version (Section 8); (d) links you choose to open in external apps, such as map locations from scanned QR codes (Section 8); and (e) the map display in the GPS Field Distance & Area Measure tool, which retrieves map imagery, transmits map-interaction data to Google, and — only if you tap the "My location" button and grant the location permission — reads a single one-off position fix from your device to move the map (Sections 8–9).

Note that exception (e) concerns map rendering only. The points, lines, and polygons you draw or tap on the map, and the distance and area measurements the App computes from them, are calculated **on your device** and are not transmitted to us or to Google as such; we receive no copy of them. Your position is read from your device only when you tap the "My location" button, is used solely to move the map camera, is not transmitted to us, and the resulting map view is stored on your device for your convenience and is never associated with any account or identifier (Sections 2, 8, and 11).

## 2. Information We Collect Ourselves
We do not operate a registration system and we collect **no name, email address, phone number, or account data**. We operate no servers of our own. If you allow crash reports or usage statistics, we receive them through Google Firebase, which processes them on our behalf (Section 5). Specifically:

* **Your files stay on your device.** Files you select, convert, scan, or generate (including camera images, scanned documents, and OCR text) are processed locally and stored only in app-private or device-shared storage you control. They are never transmitted to us.
* **No first-party tracking.** We do not set our own cookies, fingerprint your device, or build advertising profiles.
* **Settings and preferences** (e.g., language, tool options, game history/scores) are stored locally on your device using on-device storage, and are deleted if you clear the app's data or uninstall the App.
* **Your approximate or precise location, only when you ask for it.** The GPS Field Distance & Area Measure tool has a **"My location"** button. If you tap it and grant the location permission, the App asks your device's operating system for a **single, one-off** position fix, uses it for one purpose only — to move the map camera to that position — and does not add it to your measurements or transmit it to us. This is the **only** location data the App reads. **We operate no servers, so we never receive it.** We do not combine it with any account, advertising ID, or analytics identifier, do not use it for advertising or profiling, do not build a history of your movements, and do not transmit it to us or to anyone else. Because the map is then centered on your position, Google's Maps SDK requests map imagery for that area and can thereby infer it; the map's last view (which, after you use "My location", corresponds to your position) is stored on your device with your other tool settings, as described in Sections 9 and 11. See Sections 8, 9, 11, and 12 for the permission, Google's inference of the area, retention, and your rights over it.

Other data collection in the App comes from the third-party SDKs listed in Section 9, each governed by its own privacy documentation. Crash reports and usage statistics, if you allow them, are collected by us through Firebase (Section 5).

## 3. Device Identifiers and Technical Data
We do not collect advertising identifiers in the current release. Advertising-ID collection is turned off by default at the app level, and ad-personalization signals are disabled.

If and only if you explicitly consent to diagnostics and/or usage statistics (Section 5), Google's Firebase SDKs generate a pseudonymous app-instance identifier (Firebase Installation ID / FID) and Crashlytics installation identifier to manage session continuity, deliver crash reports, and measure feature usage. These identifiers are random and are not linked by us to your name, your files, or any account, but they are still personal data because they can single out a device. When you withdraw consent for usage statistics in Settings, the App immediately disables collection and clears the analytics identifiers and session state stored on your device.

If and when advertising SDKs are enabled in a future release (Section 4), advertising identifiers may be processed under the terms described in Section 4. We do not sell personal information. To the extent that sharing device identifiers with advertising partners could be characterized as a "sale" or "sharing" under California law (CCPA/CPRA), you may opt out as described in Sections 4 and 12.

## 6. Google ML Kit (On-Device Machine Learning)
The App uses the following **Google ML Kit** APIs, and input processing for all of them happens **on your device** — your images and documents are not sent to Google for recognition:

* **Text Recognition (OCR):** extracts text from images and scanned PDFs locally (Latin and other script models run on-device). Recognized text never leaves your device except as part of files you yourself choose to save or share.
* **Subject Segmentation (background removal, Android only):** runs on-device. The segmentation model is downloaded to your device over the network — at app install time via Google Play Services (declared install-time dependency) — and thereafter runs fully offline. No image content is transmitted to download or use the model.
* **Document Scanner:** on Android, ML Kit's on-device document scanner (full mode, gallery import supported); on iOS, Apple's on-device VisionKit document camera. Scanned page images stay on your device.

Note: the ML Kit SDKs themselves collect limited device/app information, performance metrics, and diagnostics for Google's own analytics (encrypted in transit, not shared with third parties by Google), as documented in Google's [ML Kit data-disclosure guidance for Google Play](https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/android-data-disclosure) and [for the App Store](https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/ios-data-disclosure). ML Kit use is subject to the [ML Kit Terms & Privacy](https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/terms).

**Google Maps Platform in detail.** Google Maps is the map provider for the GPS Field Distance & Area Measure tool; it is the App's only map source. Google automatically collects device metadata, Maps SDK and App version, IP address, a pseudonymous Maps SDK identifier used to measure SDK usage, and crash/stack-trace data — as Google's own disclosure for the [Maps SDK for Android](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/android-sdk/play-data-disclosure) describes. Because the App controls the map camera, Google additionally receives map-interaction events such as panning and zooming. Google's disclosure notes that this collected data is used by Google to improve Google services and is not shared with third parties by Google.

Three points are worth stating plainly. First, **none of this reaches us** — we operate no servers, so we do not receive, log, or store map request data or your position. Second, the SDK identifies your device only by the pseudonymous Maps SDK identifier; we do not combine it with your advertising ID, your analytics instance ID, the map view stored on your device, or any account, because we hold no accounts and build no profile of you. Third, **your location is revealed to Google only if you tap "My location" and grant the permission** — the map is then centered on your position, so the SDK requests map data for that area. Without that tap, no location is read or transmitted by this feature.

**Your position as personal data.** Because the App can read your position at your request, location is personal data about you. ## 11. Data Retention
* **Your files:** stored only on your device until you delete them; we hold no copies and cannot recover files lost to app deletion or device failure.

* **The map's stored view:** the GPS Field Distance & Area Measure tool stores its last map view (center, zoom, and rotation) locally with your other tool settings, so the map reopens where you left it. If you used "My location", that stored center corresponds to your position. The stored view is deleted when you clear the App's data, uninstall the App, or use the tool's **Restore Defaults** action. **Revoking the location permission stops any future position fix but does not by itself erase the stored map view** — clearing the App's data or using Restore Defaults does. **We hold no copy of your position at any time.** We do not set a retention period for it, because we never possess it.
* **Location data held by Google:** to the extent Google can infer your position when you tap "My location," it is retained by Google for the periods stated in [Google's Privacy Policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy). We hold none of it and cannot delete it for you; to avoid this, do not tap "My location," or revoke the permission or disable network access.
* **Google Maps request data:** retained by Google for the periods stated in [Google's Privacy Policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy). We hold none of it and cannot delete it for you; to control it, avoid opening the map tool or adjust your device's network access.
* **Analytics event data (Google Analytics for Firebase — if opted in):** event-level data is retained for **2 months**, with retention-on-new-activity turned off, and deleted automatically thereafter. Aggregated reports without identifiers may be kept longer.
* **Crash diagnostics (Firebase Crashlytics — if opted in):** retained for **90 days** by Firebase, as documented in [Firebase's privacy and security information](https://firebase.google.com/support/privacy/), after which Google begins removing it from live and backup systems.
* **Ad data (Google — future release):** retained per Google's policies linked in Section 9.
* **Purchase entitlements — if and when subscriptions are offered:** the store operators retain transaction records per their policies; on-device entitlement flags are removed when you cancel, let a subscription lapse, or uninstall the App.
